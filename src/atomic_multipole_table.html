Atomic multipole operators#

A Japanese version is here.

This page lists the atomic multipole operators \(X_{lm}^{(s)}(k)\) up to rank \(l = 2\), given by Group.atomic_multipole_operator (see Operator expression), following H. Kusunose, R. Oiwa, and S. Hayami, J. Phys. Soc. Jpn. 89, 104704 (2020).

  • Components. The real (tesseral) components are listed: \(X_{l0}\), and \(C_{lm} = [(-1)^m X_{lm} + X_{l,-m}]/\sqrt{2}\) and \(S_{lm} = [(-1)^m X_{lm} - X_{l,-m}]/(\sqrt{2}\,i)\) for \(m > 0\). They are named as the orbitals: \((x, y, z) = (C_{11}, S_{11}, C_{10})\) for \(l = 1\), and \((u, v, yz, xz, xy) = (C_{20}, C_{22}, S_{21}, C_{21}, S_{22})\) for \(l = 2\). E.g., \(Q_{u} = z^2 - (x^2 + y^2)/2\). The components are hermitian, as \(X_{l,-m} = (-1)^m X_{lm}^\dagger\) (Eqs. 6 and 14).

  • Operators. The position \(x, y, z\), the orbital angular momentum \(l_x, l_y, l_z\) (dimensionless), and the Pauli matrices \(\sigma_x, \sigma_y, \sigma_z\) (the spin is \(\boldsymbol{\sigma}/2\)), with \([l_i, l_j] = i\epsilon_{ijk} l_k\), \([\sigma_i, \sigma_j] = 2i\epsilon_{ijk}\sigma_k\), and \([l_i, x_j] = i\epsilon_{ijk} x_k\); \(x_i\) commute with each other, and \(\sigma_i\) commute with \(l_i\) and \(x_i\).

  • Order of the operators. The order of the definition is kept: in a product such as \(l_x\, y\), \(l_x\) acts after \(y\). Only a run of consecutive position operators is sorted, and the Pauli matrices are put at the right end; the commutation relations are not used otherwise, so that, e.g., \(l_x y\) and \(y l_x\) appear as separate terms.

  • Notation. \(X_{\gamma}\) for \(s = 0\) and \(X^{(1)}_{\gamma}(k)\) for \(s = 1\), with the name of the component \(\gamma\) (e.g., \(Q_{u}\) and \(Q^{(1)}_{x}(0)\)), and \(X_{0}\) and \(X^{(1)}_{0}(k)\) for rank 0. The matrix elements in an orbital basis are those of the atomic SAMB before the symmetry adaptation and the normalization, with the radial factor \(\langle r^k \rangle = 1\).

  • Rank 1 in vectors. The three components of rank 1 are written together as a vector, e.g., \(\boldsymbol{T} = (T_{x}, T_{y}, T_{z})\) and \(\boldsymbol{Q}^{(1)}(0) = (Q^{(1)}_{x}(0), Q^{(1)}_{y}(0), Q^{(1)}_{z}(0))\), with \(\boldsymbol{r} = (x, y, z)\), \(\boldsymbol{l} = (l_x, l_y, l_z)\) and \(\boldsymbol{\sigma} = (\sigma_x, \sigma_y, \sigma_z)\). The order of the operators in the products is kept also here, e.g., \((\boldsymbol{r} \times \boldsymbol{l})_x = y l_z - z l_y\) and \((\boldsymbol{l} \times \boldsymbol{r})_x = l_y z - l_z y\). The scalars of rank 0 are written in the same way. The terms with \(\boldsymbol{r} \cdot \boldsymbol{l}\) or \(\boldsymbol{l} \cdot \boldsymbol{r}\) are omitted, as they vanish (\(\boldsymbol{r} \cdot \boldsymbol{l} = \boldsymbol{l} \cdot \boldsymbol{r} = 0\) for \(\boldsymbol{l} = -i\,\boldsymbol{r} \times \nabla\)); Group.atomic_multipole_operator gives them, as it does not use the commutation relations.

The vanishing ones (e.g., \(M_{0}\) and \(\boldsymbol{G}\) of \(s = 0\)) are not listed.

Electric (Q)#

\[ \begin{aligned} Q_{0} &= 1 \end{aligned} \]
\[ \begin{aligned} \boldsymbol{Q} &= \boldsymbol{r} \end{aligned} \]
\[\begin{split} \begin{aligned} Q_{u} &= - \frac{x^{2}}{2} - \frac{y^{2}}{2} + z^{2} \\ Q_{v} &= \frac{\sqrt{3} x^{2}}{2} - \frac{\sqrt{3} y^{2}}{2} \\ Q_{yz} &= \sqrt{3} y z \\ Q_{xz} &= \sqrt{3} x z \\ Q_{xy} &= \sqrt{3} x y \end{aligned} \end{split}\]
