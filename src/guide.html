User Guide#

This guide explains the quantities that MultiPie provides and how to read and use them: the multipole key and its symbol, the point-group harmonics, the atomic orbitals, the atomic, cluster, and combined SAMB (symmetry-adapted multipole basis), the tight-binding model built from them, and the response tensors. A Japanese version is here.

The group data come from the group database generated by MultiPieGen; the tables of all groups are in Group Information. The code examples use Group (the group data) and MaterialModel (a model):

from multipie import Group, MaterialModel

1. Multipole key#

2. Point-group harmonics#

Spherical and tesseral harmonics. The harmonics of rank \(l\) are built from the homogeneous polynomials

\[ O_{lm}(\boldsymbol{r}) = \sqrt{\frac{4\pi}{2l+1}}\, r^l\, Y_{lm}(\theta, \phi), \qquad m = l, l-1, \dots, -l, \]

e.g., \(O_{10} = z\), \(O_{1,\pm1} = \mp(x \pm iy)/\sqrt{2}\). Their real combinations are the tesseral harmonics, e.g., \(C_{11} = x\), \(S_{11} = y\), \(C_{22} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}(x^2 - y^2)\), \(S_{22} = \sqrt{3}xy\).

Point-group harmonics. A harmonics (X, l, Gamma, n) of a point group is a set of rank-\(l\) functions that forms a basis of the irrep \(\Gamma\). Its component \(\gamma\) is

\[ X_{l\Gamma n}^{(\gamma)}(\boldsymbol{r}) = \sum_{m} U_{m\gamma}\, O_{lm}(\boldsymbol{r}), \]

with the unitary matrix \(U\) ((2l+1) x dim, rows \(m = l, \dots, -l\)). Group.harmonics gives, for each key, the polynomials of the components and \(U\):

g = Group("Oh")
ex, U = g.harmonics[("Q", 1, "T1u", -1, -1, 0, 0, "q")]
ex

array([x, y, z], dtype=object)

U

array([[-sqrt(2)/2, sqrt(2)*I/2, 0],
       [0, 0, 1],
       [sqrt(2)/2, sqrt(2)*I/2, 0]], dtype=object)

  • The harmonics are given up to rank 11, for Q (polar) and G (axial). An axial harmonics transforms with the extra factor \(\det R\) under an improper operation \(R\), so that Q and G of the same function can belong to different irreps, e.g., in C\(_{\rm 4v}\), \(\mathbb{Q}_{1}(A_{1}) = z\) but \(\mathbb{G}_{1}(A_{2}) = z\). In a group of proper rotations only, Q and G are the same.

  • When an irrep appears more than once at the same rank, n = 1, 2, ... distinguishes the harmonics, e.g., in C\(_{\rm 4h}\), \(\mathbb{Q}_{2}(B_{g},1) = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}(x^2 - y^2)\) and \(\mathbb{Q}_{2}(B_{g},2) = \sqrt{3}xy\).

  • The harmonics of a space group or a magnetic group are those of its point group.

In a model input, the orbitals of a site are given by these names, e.g., "d" (all five), ["dyz", "dxz", "dxy"], or "(3/2,p)" for jml. A spinful model ("spinful": True) uses lgs (or jml).

4. Atomic SAMB#

The atomic SAMB are the matrices of the atomic multipole operators in the orbital basis, adapted to the point group. They describe the on-site degrees of freedom of the electrons of an atom: the charge, the orbital (quadrupole) order, the orbital angular momentum, the spin, and so on.

  • The multipoles \(X_{lm}^{(s)}(k)\) of X = Q, G, T, M with s = 0 (orbital) and s = 1 (spin-dependent), symmetry-adapted with \(U\) of the harmonics, \(X_{l\Gamma n}^{(s)}(k)[\gamma] = \sum_m U_{m\gamma} X_{lm}^{(s)}(k)\).

  • Each component has the norm 1 in a diagonal block (\(L_1 = L_2\)), and \(1/\sqrt{2}\) in an off-diagonal block.

Group.atomic_samb(basis_type, (L1, L2)) gives, for each key, the matrices of the components, (dim, n1, n2), and the expression of the harmonics:

a = g.atomic_samb("lg", (1, 1))  # p-p block of Oh.
list(a.keys())

[('Q', 0, 'A1g', -1, -1, 0, 0, 'q'),
 ('Q', 2, 'Eg', -1, -1, 0, 0, 'q'),
 ('Q', 2, 'T2g', -1, -1, 0, 0, 'q'),
 ('M', 1, 'T1g', -1, -1, 0, 0, 'q')]

mat, ex = a[("M", 1, "T1g", -1, -1, 0, 0, "q")]
mat[0]  # x component, l_x / sqrt(2) in (px, py, pz).

array([[0, 0, 0],
       [0, 0, -sqrt(2)*I/2],
       [0, sqrt(2)*I/2, 0]], dtype=object)

For the p orbitals of O\(_{\rm h}\): \(\mathbb{Q}_{0}^{(a)}(A_{1g})\) is the charge (\(\propto\) identity), \(\mathbb{Q}_{2}^{(a)}(E_{g})\) and \(\mathbb{Q}_{2}^{(a)}(T_{2g})\) the orbital quadrupoles, and \(\mathbb{M}_{1}^{(a)}(T_{1g})\) the orbital angular momentum. With the spin (lgs), the spin magnetic dipole is \(\mathbb{M}_{1}^{(1,-1;a)}(T_{1g})\), and the spin-orbit coupling \(\boldsymbol{l} \cdot \boldsymbol{s}\) of the p orbitals is proportional to \(\mathbb{Q}_{0}^{(1,1;a)}(A_{1g})\).

Operator expression. Group.atomic_multipole_operator(X, l, m, s=0, k=0) gives the atomic multipole \(X_{lm}^{(s)}(k)\) of a component \(m\) of the spherical basis as an operator, following H. Kusunose, R. Oiwa, and S. Hayami, J. Phys. Soc. Jpn. 89, 104704 (2020). The spinless ones (Eqs. 1-4) are, with \(\boldsymbol{l} = -i(\boldsymbol{r} \times \nabla)\) and \(\nabla\) acting only on \(O_{lm}(\boldsymbol{r}) = \sqrt{4\pi/(2l+1)}\, r^l Y_{lm}(\hat{\boldsymbol{r}})\),

\[\begin{split} \begin{aligned} Q_{lm} &= O_{lm}, \\ M_{lm} &= \frac{1}{l+1}\,[(\nabla O_{lm})\cdot\boldsymbol{l} + \boldsymbol{l}\cdot(\nabla O_{lm})], \\ T_{lm} &= \frac{1}{(l+1)(l+2)}\,[(\nabla O_{lm})\cdot(\boldsymbol{r}\times\boldsymbol{l}) - (\boldsymbol{l}\times\boldsymbol{r})\cdot(\nabla O_{lm})], \\ G_{lm} &= \frac{2i}{(l+1)^2(l+2)}\,[(\nabla O_{lm})\cdot\boldsymbol{l}\,\boldsymbol{l}^2 - \boldsymbol{l}^2\,\boldsymbol{l}\cdot(\nabla O_{lm})], \end{aligned} \end{split}\]

and the spin-dependent ones (Eq. 13) couple an orbital multipole \(X'\) of rank \(l + k\) with the Pauli matrices,

\[\begin{split} X_{lm}^{(1)}(k) = i^{1+k}(-1)^{l+m}\sqrt{2l+1}\sum_{n} \begin{pmatrix} l+k & l & 1\\ m-n & -m & n\end{pmatrix} X'_{l+k,m-n}\,\sigma_{1,n}, \end{split}\]

with \(\sigma_{1,0} = \sigma_z\) and \(\sigma_{1,\pm1} = \mp(\sigma_x \pm i\sigma_y)/\sqrt2\). \(X'\) is T, G, Q, M for X = Q, M, T, G with k = 0, and M, Q, G, T with k = ±1 (Table I of the paper). Unlike the multipolar harmonics, whose orbital part is always polar, the orbital part \(X'\) here can be polar (Q, T) or axial (G, M, e.g., the orbital angular momentum).

  • The result is a sympy expression in the noncommutative operators: the position x, y, z, the orbital angular momentum l_x, l_y, l_z (dimensionless), and the Pauli matrices sigma_x, sigma_y, sigma_z (\(\sigma_0 = 1\); the spin is \(\boldsymbol{\sigma}/2\)).

  • They obey \([l_i, l_j] = i\epsilon_{ijk} l_k\), \([\sigma_i, \sigma_j] = 2i\epsilon_{ijk}\sigma_k\), and \([l_i, x_j] = i\epsilon_{ijk} x_k\); \(x_i\) commute with each other, and \(\sigma_i\) commute with \(l_i\) and \(x_i\).

  • The order of the operators in the definition is kept. Only a run of consecutive position operators is sorted as \(x, y, z\), and the Pauli matrices are moved to the right end; no other commutation relation is used. \(G_{lm}\) with \(l < 2\) is 0.

  • The operator acts on any orbitals, including those of different ranks (e.g., p-d), through the position. Its matrix elements are those of Group.atomic_samb before the symmetry adaptation and the normalization, with the radial factor \(\langle r^k \rangle = 1\).

Group.atomic_multipole_operator("Q", 0, 0, s=1, k=1)  # spin-orbit coupling, l.sigma / sqrt(3).
\[\displaystyle \frac{\sqrt{3} l_{x} \sigma_{x}}{3} + \frac{\sqrt{3} l_{y} \sigma_{y}}{3} + \frac{\sqrt{3} l_{z} \sigma_{z}}{3}\]
Group.atomic_multipole_operator("M", 1, 0)  # orbital angular momentum, l_z.
\[\displaystyle l_{z}\]
Group.atomic_multipole_operator("T", 1, 0)  # magnetic toroidal dipole, [(r x l) - (l x r)]_z / 6.
\[\displaystyle - \frac{l_{x} y}{6} + \frac{l_{y} x}{6} + \frac{x l_{y}}{6} - \frac{y l_{x}}{6}\]

The operators up to rank 2 are listed in Atomic multipole operators. A runnable script is docs/src/examples/atomic_multipole_operator.py.

5. Cluster SAMB#

The cluster SAMB of a Wyckoff position are functions on its \(N\) equivalent sites (or bonds) that form bases of the irreps of the group. They describe how a quantity is distributed over the sites or the bonds of a cluster.

  • Site. Functions on the \(N\) sites of a site Wyckoff position (e.g., 2a). They are real, of type Q, and form a complete orthonormal set.

  • Bond. Functions on the \(N\) bonds of a bond Wyckoff position. The label 2b@1o is the bond Wyckoff position 2b (2 bonds) whose centers are at the site Wyckoff position 1o. The symmetric bond functions (the same value for the two directions of a bond, a real hopping) have the type Q or G and real values; the anti-symmetric ones (opposite signs for the two directions, an imaginary hopping) have the type T or M and pure imaginary values.

Group.cluster_samb(wp) and Group.cluster_samb(wp, "bond") give, for each key, the values on the sites or bonds, (dim, N), and the expression of the harmonics. For D\(_{\rm 4h}\):

d = Group("D4h")
d.find_wyckoff_site("[0,0,1/4]")[0]  # two sites at (0, 0, ±z).

'2a'

for key, (samb, ex) in d.cluster_samb("2a").items(): print(key[:3], samb, ex)

('Q', 0, 'A1g') [[sqrt(2)/2 sqrt(2)/2]] [1]
('Q', 1, 'A2u') [[sqrt(2)/2 -sqrt(2)/2]] [z]

for key, (samb, ex) in d.cluster_samb("2b@1o", "bond").items(): print(key[:5], samb.tolist())

('Q', 0, 'A1g', -1, -1) [[sqrt(2)/2, sqrt(2)/2]]
('Q', 2, 'B1g', -1, -1) [[-sqrt(2)/2, sqrt(2)/2]]
('T', 1, 'Eu', -1, 1) [[I, 0], [0, I]]

The bonds of 2b@1o are along \(x\) and \(y\) through the origin: \(\mathbb{Q}_{0}^{(b)}(A_{1g})\) is the uniform real hopping, \(\mathbb{Q}_{2}^{(b)}(B_{1g})\) the real hopping with opposite signs on the two bonds (\(x^2 - y^2\) symmetry), and \(\mathbb{T}_{1}^{(b)}(E_{u},a)\) the imaginary hoppings with the symmetry of \((x, y)\), a current along each bond.

6. Combined SAMB and the model#

A tight-binding model is written by the combined SAMB, products of an atomic SAMB (on-site or bra-ket orbitals) and a cluster SAMB (sites or bonds) combined into an irrep with the Clebsch-Gordan coefficients:

\[ Z_j = \sum_{\gamma_1 \gamma_2} C^{\gamma}_{\gamma_1 \gamma_2}\, X^{(\mathrm{a})}_{\gamma_1} \otimes Y^{(\mathrm{s/b})}_{\gamma_2}, \qquad H = \sum_j z_j Z_j. \]

MaterialModel.analyze(model_input) (or mp_create) builds all of them for the sites, orbitals, and bonds of the model. The SAMB of the identity irrep, of type Q and G (time-reversal even), are those allowed in a Hamiltonian; "SAMB_select": {"Gamma": "IR"} selects them. For graphene (two C \(p_z\) sites, bonds up to the second neighbor):

mm = MaterialModel()
mm.analyze({"model": "graphene", "group": 191, "cell": {"c": 4}, "site": {"C": ("[1/3,2/3,0]", "pz")},
            "bond": [("C", "C", [1, 2])], "SAMB_select": {"Gamma": "IR"}, "pdf": {"create": False}})
for z, (symbol, u, key, comp) in mm["combined_id"].items(): print(z, symbol, u.samb_type.wyckoff, u.neighbor)

z1 \mathbb{Q}_{0}^{(c)}(A_{1g}) 2c 0
z2 \mathbb{Q}_{0}^{(c)}(A_{1g}) 3a@3f 1
z3 \mathbb{Q}_{0}^{(c)}(A_{1g}) 6b@6l 2

  • mm["combined_id"] gives, for each \(z_j\), its symbol, the site or bond cluster with its neighbor order (u.samb_type and u.neighbor), the key, and the component.

  • mm["combined_samb"] gives the definition of each combined SAMB: for each atomic and cluster block, the list of (coefficient, atomic key, atomic component, cluster key, cluster component).

  • mm.get_combined_samb_matrix(fmt="value") gives the matrix of each \(Z_j\) in real space, with the keys (n1, n2, n3, m, n): the lattice vector \(\boldsymbol{R} = (n_1, n_2, n_3)\) and the bra and ket indices of mm["full_matrix"]["ket"]. E.g., the nearest-neighbor hopping \(Z_2\) of graphene has the elements \(1/\sqrt{6}\) (6 bonds, normalized).

  • The PDF of the model (mp_create with "pdf": {"create": True}) lists all the SAMB with their definitions.

The weights \(z_j\) are given in the control of ModelAnalyzer (or mp_analyze), {"samb": {"parameter": {"z1": ...}}}. Since each \(Z_j\) is normalized, \(z_j\) has the unit of energy. The numbering of \(z_j\) depends on the group database (see Changes from Ver.2); the space spanned by the SAMB does not. See Examples of materials for complete examples.

7. Response tensors#

A response tensor of rank 0 to 4 relates two physical quantities, e.g., a current and an electric field. For each of the 157 magnetic point groups, Group.response_tensor_all() gives the tensors allowed by symmetry, as a dict with the keys (X, rank, opt) (only the type X with Group.response_tensor_all(X)).

For example, the electric conductivity \(\sigma_{ab}\) (\(j_a = \sigma_{ab} E_b\)) is a rank-2 tensor; its symmetric part (s) is the ordinary conductivity, and its anti-symmetric part (a, odd under the time reversal) the Hall conductivity, which is carried by the magnetic dipole. The magneto-electric tensor \(\alpha_{ab}\) (\(P_a = \alpha_{ab} H_b\)) is axial and odd under the time reversal (M): its symmetric part is carried by the magnetic monopole and the magnetic quadrupoles, and its anti-symmetric part by the magnetic toroidal dipole. The Edelstein (current-induced magnetization) tensor \(\chi_{ab}\) (\(M_a = \chi_{ab} j_b\)) is axial and even under the time reversal (G): its symmetric part is carried by the electric toroidal monopole and quadrupoles, as in a chiral crystal, and its anti-symmetric part by the electric dipole, as in a polar crystal.

Why these types and multipoles. The type X of a response tensor follows from the parities of the two quantities it relates. Under the spatial inversion, the electric field \(\boldsymbol{E}\), the current \(\boldsymbol{j}\) and the polarization \(\boldsymbol{P}\) are polar vectors, and the magnetic field \(\boldsymbol{H}\) and the magnetization \(\boldsymbol{M}\) are axial vectors. Under the time reversal, \(\boldsymbol{E}\) and \(\boldsymbol{P}\) are even, and \(\boldsymbol{j}\), \(\boldsymbol{H}\) and \(\boldsymbol{M}\) are odd. The tensor that relates a response to a field has the product of their parities:

For the conductivity, the time reversal is not a symmetry of the response itself, which dissipates energy. The Onsager relation, \(\sigma_{ab}(\boldsymbol{X}) = \sigma_{ba}(-\boldsymbol{X})\) for the order parameters \(\boldsymbol{X}\) odd under the time reversal (e.g., the magnetization), divides it into two parts. The symmetric part, the dissipative (ohmic) conductivity proportional to the relaxation time \(\tau\), is even in \(\boldsymbol{X}\): as a property of the material, apart from the factor \(\tau\) (odd under the time reversal, as the dissipation itself), it is even (Q), and is present in every material. The anti-symmetric part, the non-dissipative Hall conductivity, is odd in \(\boldsymbol{X}\) (T), and needs the time reversal broken, by a magnetization of the material or by a magnetic field. In this sense, X is the symmetry that a material must have for the response, i.e., the type of the multipoles that carry it.

The multipoles then follow from the decomposition of a rank-2 tensor \(X_{ab}\) into irreducible parts: the trace (rank 0), the anti-symmetric part, which is a vector \(\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{abc} X_{bc}\) (rank 1), and the symmetric traceless part (rank 2). The parts of rank 0 and 2 have the type of the tensor. The vector of the anti-symmetric part has the opposite parity under the spatial inversion, as \(\epsilon_{abc}\) is axial, and the same parity under the time reversal:

This is why the Hall effect (T, anti-symmetric) needs a magnetic dipole (a magnetization or a magnetic field), the magneto-electric effect a magnetic monopole or quadrupole (symmetric) or a magnetic toroidal dipole (anti-symmetric), and the Edelstein effect an electric toroidal monopole or quadrupole (symmetric; a chirality) or an electric dipole (anti-symmetric; a polarity). The tensors of higher rank are decomposed in the same way.

Active multipoles. Each tensor component is decomposed into multipoles (irreducible cartesian parts). Only the active multipoles of the group, those invariant under all its operations including the time reversal, can be nonzero. This gives the nonvanishing components and the relations among them. Group.active_multipole lists them, named by X + an orbital name, e.g., Qdu; multipole_rank gives the rank and convert_mp_alias the alias of a name (both in multipie.util.util_response_tensor), e.g., Qdu has the rank 2 and the alias Qu.

8. Multipolar harmonics#

A multipolar harmonics combines a point-group harmonics with an internal degree of freedom, e.g., a displacement, an electric polarization, or a spin at each point.

Internal degree of freedom. The internal degree of freedom is fixed by its rank s (s = 1: a vector; s = 2, 3: higher-rank tensors) and its type x: polar (x = q, e.g., a displacement or an electric polarization) or axial (x = g, e.g., a spin or a rotation). Its basis \(e_\nu\) is the set of the rank-s orbitals of that type, of the cubic family (O\(_{\rm h}\)) or of the hexagonal family (D\(_{\rm 6h}\)), written with the symbols \(Q_\nu\) (x = q) or \(G_\nu\) (x = g), e.g., \((Q_x, Q_y, Q_z)\) or \((G_x, G_y, G_z)\) for s = 1. The bases are in Group.global_info()["harmonics"]["internal_basis"], by the family ("cubic" or "hexagonal") and (s, x).

Multipolar spherical harmonics. The multipolar spherical harmonics of total rank \(l\) couple the orbital part of rank \(l + k\) with the internal basis:

\[\begin{split} O_{lm}^{(s)}(k) = i^{k+s} (-1)^{l+m} \sqrt{2l+1} \sum_{\nu} \begin{pmatrix} l+k & l & s \\ m-\nu & -m & \nu \end{pmatrix} O_{l+k, m-\nu}\, e_\nu, \qquad k = -s, \dots, s. \end{split}\]

Important

The orbital part \(O_{l+k, m-\nu}(\boldsymbol{r}) = \sqrt{4\pi/(2l+2k+1)}\, r^{l+k} Y_{l+k, m-\nu}(\hat{\boldsymbol{r}})\) is always a polar function of \(\boldsymbol{r}\). Therefore, the type X (polar Q or axial G) is fixed by the internal type x and the parity of s + k, as in the table below. This is the definition of the multipolar harmonics in MultiPie, and it differs from that of the atomic multipoles of H. Kusunose, R. Oiwa, and S. Hayami, J. Phys. Soc. Jpn. 89, 104704 (2020), whose orbital part can be polar (Q, T) or axial (G, M, e.g., the orbital angular momentum).

Type of the multipole. For a fixed internal type x, the type X of the multipole is given by the parity of s + k:

That is, X has the same polar or axial character as the internal basis if s + k is even, and the opposite one if s + k is odd. The multipolar harmonics of a point group is \(\sum_m U_{m\gamma} O_{lm}^{(s)}(k)\), with \(U\) of the point-group harmonics (X, l, Gamma, n).

Magnetic internal degree of freedom. The internal bases \(Q_\nu\) and \(G_\nu\) are even under the time reversal. For a magnetic internal degree of freedom, odd under the time reversal, e.g., a spin or a magnetic moment, read the types as Q \(\to\) T and G \(\to\) M: the polar or axial character is unchanged, and the multipole becomes odd under the time reversal. For example, with a spin \(\boldsymbol{\sigma}\) as the axial vector \(\boldsymbol{G}\), \(\boldsymbol{r} \cdot \boldsymbol{G}\) (\(\vec{\mathbb{G}}_{0}^{(1,1)}[g]\)) is the magnetic monopole \(\boldsymbol{r} \cdot \boldsymbol{\sigma}\), and \(\boldsymbol{G} \times \boldsymbol{r}\) (\(\vec{\mathbb{Q}}_{1}^{(1,0)}[g]\)) the magnetic toroidal dipole \(\boldsymbol{\sigma} \times \boldsymbol{r}\).

Key and value. The key is PGMultipoleType(X, l, Gamma, n, p, s, k, x) with s = 1, 2, 3 and l <= 3, for all 47 point groups. Group.multipolar_harmonics gives, for each key, the coefficients of the internal basis (dim x the number of the internal basis functions, polynomials in \(x, y, z\)) and the expression in terms of the internal basis symbols. The symbol has an arrow and the superscript \((s, k)\), and the internal type is written as \([q]\) or \([g]\), e.g., \(\vec{\mathbb{G}}_{1}^{(1,0)}[q](T_{1g})\); Group.tag_multipole(key, latex=True, vector=True) gives the symbol with the arrow.

Example: spin-orbit coupling depending on the wave vector. Reading the axial vector \(\boldsymbol{G}\) as the spin \(\boldsymbol{\sigma}\) and the coordinates \((x, y, z)\) as the wave vector \((k_x, k_y, k_z)\), the multipolar harmonics with x = g and s = 1 give the spin-orbit couplings of the form \(\boldsymbol{k}\)-\(\boldsymbol{\sigma}\). Expression for a component. Group.multipolar_harmonics_expression(X, l, m, s=0, k=0, x="q", internal="cubic") gives \(O_{lm}^{(s)}(k)\) itself for a component \(m\) of the spherical basis (\(-l \le m \le l\)): a polynomial in \(x, y, z\) multiplied by the internal basis symbols. It is not limited to \(l \le 3\), and the internal rank is s = 0, 1, 2, 3 (k = -s, ..., s, l + k >= 0), with the internal basis of the cubic ("cubic") or hexagonal ("hexagonal") family.

  • Both X (Q, G, T, M) and x are given. As the orbital part is always polar, a combination of X and x of the wrong parity (not in the table above) gives 0.

  • For X = T, M, the internal symbols are renamed Q \( o\) T and G \( o\) M (magnetic internal degree of freedom).

  • For s = 0, the internal symbol is multiplied only for \(G_{0}\) and \(M_{0}\) (X = G, M with x = g); for \(Q_{0}\) and \(T_{0}\), the polynomial alone is given, as in Group.harmonics.

  • The multipolar harmonics of a point group is \(\sum_m U_{m\gamma} O_{lm}^{(s)}(k)\) with \(U\) of Group.harmonics (for T/M, that of Q/G).
