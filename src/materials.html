Examples of materials#

A Japanese version is here.

This page builds tight-binding models of three materials with MultiPie, step by step: SrVO\(_3\) (a cubic perovskite), monolayer MoS\(_2\) (a hexagonal layer without inversion), and Te (a chiral crystal with the spin-orbit coupling). They cover the main ingredients of a model: several sites with different orbitals, bonds between different sites up to further neighbors, a point group whose irreps carry primes (D\(_{\rm 3h}\)), and a spinful model in a chiral group.

The same model inputs are used by the tests (tests/test_materials.py), which check the properties described at the end of each section, see Checks by symmetry. The outputs on this page are created by docs/src/examples/materials/create_examples.py.

The weights \(z_j\) used for the band dispersions below are chosen only for illustration; they are not fitted to any first-principles calculation. To build a realistic model, fit them to a calculation, e.g., with the symcw mode of mp_analyze (see Post processing).

Overview of the procedure#

For each material:

  1. Write a model input file, <name>_in.py. It gives the space group, the cell, the sites with their orbitals, and the bonds by neighbor. "SAMB_select": {"Gamma": "IR"} selects the SAMB of the identity irrep, which are the ones allowed in a Hamiltonian.

  2. Create the model,

    mp_create SrVO3_in.py

    which writes SrVO3/SrVO3.pkl (the model) and SrVO3/SrVO3.pdf (a summary with all the SAMB and their definitions).

  3. Choose the weights \(z_j\) of the SAMB \(Z_j\) in a control file, <name>_ctrl.py. The Hamiltonian is

    \[ H = \sum_j z_j Z_j, \]

    where each \(Z_j\) is a hermitian matrix normalized to 1, so that \(z_j\) has the unit of energy.

  4. Analyze the model,

    mp_analyze SrVO3_ctrl.py

    which writes the band dispersion along the k path to SrVO3/output/.

In the tables of SAMB below, “site/bond” gives the sites of the bra and the ket, “Wyckoff” the Wyckoff position of the site or the bond (12b@12j is a bond Wyckoff position 12b with the bond centers at 12j), “neighbor” the order of the neighbor of the bond (- for an on-site term), and “bra-ket” the orbital ranks. The symbol is the combined SAMB, \(\mathbb{X}_{l}^{(c)}(\Gamma)\), with the type X, the rank l, and the irrep \(\Gamma\); how to read it is explained in the User Guide. The expression of each \(Z_j\) in terms of the atomic and the cluster SAMB is given in the PDF of the model.

SrVO\(_3\): cubic perovskite#

SrVO\(_3\) has the cubic perovskite structure (space group \(Pm\bar{3}m\), No. 221, \(a = 3.84\) Å). The conduction bands come from the V \(t_{2g}\) orbitals (\(d_{yz}\), \(d_{xz}\), \(d_{xy}\)), which hybridize with the O \(p\) orbitals. We take the V site at the body center (Wyckoff 1b) and the three O sites at the face centers (3c), and omit Sr.

# SrVO3_in.py: cubic perovskite (Pm-3m), V t2g and O p orbitals (Sr is omitted).
SrVO3_in = {
    "model": "SrVO3",
    "group": 221,  # Pm-3m.
    "cell": {"a": 3.84},
    #
    "site": {
        "V": ("[1/2,1/2,1/2]", ["dyz", "dxz", "dxy"]),  # 1b, t2g.
        "O": ("[1/2,1/2,0]", "p"),  # 3c.
    },
    "bond": [("V", "O", [1]), ("O", "O", [1]), ("V", "V", [1])],
    #
    "spinful": False,
    "SAMB_select": {"Gamma": "IR"},  # identity irrep, for the Hamiltonian.
}

The number of SAMB of each kind follows from the symmetry:

  • V on-site (\(z_3\)): the \(t_{2g}\) orbitals form the irrep \(T_{2g}\) of the site symmetry O\(_{\rm h}\), so one energy.

  • O on-site (\(z_1\), \(z_2\)): the site symmetry of O is D\(_{\rm 4h}\), where the \(p\) orbitals split into the one along the V-O bond and the two perpendicular to it, so two energies.

  • V-O hopping (\(z_8\)): a \(t_{2g}\) orbital has no \(\sigma\) bond with \(p\), so only the \(pd\pi\) hopping remains.

  • V-V hopping (\(z_9\), \(z_{10}\)): \(dd\pi\) and \(dd\delta\) between \(t_{2g}\) orbitals.

  • O-O hopping (\(z_4\)-\(z_7\)): four parameters, as the O-O bond has a low symmetry.

The control gives the O on-site energy, the V-O hopping, and an O-O hopping:

# SrVO3_ctrl.py: illustrative weights (not fitted), on-site energies and nearest-neighbor V-O hopping.
SrVO3_ctrl = {
    "samb": {
        "model": "SrVO3",
        "parameter": {
            "z1": -12.0,  # O p on-site (uniform).
            "z3": 0.0,  # V t2g on-site.
            "z8": 3.0,  # V-O hopping (t2g-p pi bond).
            "z4": 0.5,  # O-O nearest-neighbor hopping.
        },
    },
    "output": {
        "dispersion": {
            "k_path": "Γ-X-M-Γ-R-X",
            "k_point": {"Γ": "[0,0,0]", "X": "[1/2,0,0]", "M": "[1/2,1/2,0]", "R": "[1/2,1/2,1/2]"},
        }
    },
}

The three \(t_{2g}\) bands lie above the nine O \(p\) bands. With the \(pd\pi\) hopping alone, a \(t_{2g}\) orbital hops only in its own plane, e.g., \(d_{yz}\) only within the \(yz\) plane, so one of the \(t_{2g}\) bands is flat along \(\Gamma\)-X. At \(\Gamma\) and R, the \(t_{2g}\) bands are triply degenerate (\(T_{2g}\)), and so are the O \(p\) bands at \(\Gamma\) (\(2T_{1u} + T_{2u}\)).

MoS\(_2\): monolayer of 2H-MoS\(_2\)#

A monolayer of 2H-MoS\(_2\) has the space group \(P\bar{6}m2\) (No. 187, point group D\(_{\rm 3h}\)), without the inversion. The Mo site is at 1a, and the two S sites above and below it are at 2h, \((1/3, 2/3, \pm z)\). A large \(c\) (20 Å) separates the layers. We take the five Mo \(d\) orbitals and the three S \(p\) orbitals.

# MoS2_in.py: monolayer 2H-MoS2 (P-6m2, D3h), Mo d and S p orbitals.
MoS2_in = {
    "model": "MoS2",
    "group": 187,  # P-6m2.
    "cell": {"a": 3.16, "c": 20.0},  # vacuum along c.
    #
    "site": {
        "Mo": ("[0,0,0]", "d"),  # 1a.
        "S": ("[1/3,2/3,0.078]", "p"),  # 2h, z = 1.56/20.
    },
    "bond": [("Mo", "S", [1]), ("Mo", "Mo", [1]), ("S", "S", [1, 2])],
    #
    "spinful": False,
    "SAMB_select": {"Gamma": "IR"},  # identity irrep, for the Hamiltonian.
}

The irreps of D\(_{\rm 3h}\) are written with primes, e.g., \(A_1'\) (the identity irrep) and \(E''\). From Ver.3, the irreps are in the order \(A_1', A_2', E', A_1'', A_2'', E''\) (see Changes from Ver.2). The site symmetry splits the Mo \(d\) orbitals into \(A_1'\) (\(d_{z^2}\)), \(E'\) (\(d_{x^2-y^2}\), \(d_{xy}\)), and \(E''\) (\(d_{xz}\), \(d_{yz}\)), giving the three on-site energies \(z_1\)-\(z_3\); the S \(p\) orbitals split into \(p_z\) and (\(p_x\), \(p_y\)), giving \(z_4\) and \(z_5\).

# MoS2_ctrl.py: illustrative weights (not fitted).
MoS2_ctrl = {
    "samb": {
        "model": "MoS2",
        "parameter": {
            "z2": 1.0,  # Mo d crystal field.
            "z4": -6.0,  # S p on-site.
            "z15": 3.0,  # Mo-S hopping.
            "z16": 1.0,  # Mo-S hopping.
            "z6": -0.5,  # Mo-Mo hopping.
        },
    },
    "output": {"dispersion": {"k_path": "Γ-M-K-Γ", "k_point": {"Γ": "[0,0,0]", "M": "[1/2,0,0]", "K": "[1/3,1/3,0]"}}},
}

At \(\Gamma\), the bands are non-degenerate (\(A_1'\), \(A_2''\)) or doubly degenerate (\(E'\), \(E''\)): three single bands and four pairs for the eleven bands.

Te: a chiral crystal with the spin-orbit coupling#

Trigonal Te has the chiral space group \(P3_121\) (No. 152, point group D\(_3\)): the Te atoms form helical chains along \(c\), and the crystal has no mirror and no inversion. The three Te sites are at 3a, \((x, 0, 1/3)\) with \(x = 0.2636\). We take the \(p\) orbitals with the spin ("spinful": True), and the bonds within a chain (first neighbor) and between the chains (second neighbor).

# Te_in.py: trigonal Te (P3_121, chiral D3), spinful p orbitals.
Te_in = {
    "model": "Te",
    "group": 152,  # P3_121.
    "cell": {"a": 4.45, "c": 5.93},
    #
    "site": {"Te": ("[0.2636,0,1/3]", "p")},  # 3a.
    "bond": [("Te", "Te", [1, 2])],  # intra- and inter-chain.
    #
    "spinful": True,
    "SAMB_select": {"Gamma": "IR"},  # identity irrep, for the Hamiltonian (Q and G).
}

Three features show up:

  • Spin-dependent SAMB. In a spinful model, the atomic SAMB include the spin, written with the superscript \((s, k)\) with \(s = 1\). For example, \(z_6\), \(\mathbb{Q}_{0}^{(1,1;c)}(A_1)\), is the on-site (symmetric) spin-orbit coupling, proportional to \(\boldsymbol{l} \cdot \boldsymbol{s}\).

  • Electric toroidal (G) SAMB. As D\(_3\) has only rotations, the axial (G) SAMB of the identity irrep are allowed in the Hamiltonian together with the polar (Q) ones, e.g., \(\mathbb{G}_{0}^{(c)}(A_1)\) (\(z_{19}\), \(z_{20}\)), the electric toroidal monopole of the bonds, which represents the chirality.

  • Antisymmetric spin-orbit coupling. \(z_{22}\) and \(z_{23}\), \(\mathbb{G}_{0}^{(1,-1;c)}(A_1)\), combine the spin \(\sigma_z\) (\(z_{22}\)) or \(\sigma_x, \sigma_y\) (\(z_{23}\)), independent of the orbitals, with the anti-symmetric (pure imaginary) bond function of the first neighbor: a spin-dependent hopping odd under the exchange of the two ends of a bond, \(\propto i\,\boldsymbol{\sigma} \cdot \boldsymbol{d}\) along a bond \(\boldsymbol{d}\). It is the spin-orbit coupling allowed by the chirality (a G monopole, i.e., a pseudoscalar), which gives \(\boldsymbol{\sigma} \cdot \boldsymbol{k}\) near \(\Gamma\).

The spin splitting of the bands needs a term odd under the inversion, i.e., a G SAMB of the identity irrep of D\(_3\) (which belongs to \(A_{1u}\) of D\(_{3d}\)). The on-site spin-orbit coupling \(z_6\) is even, so that with \(z_6\) and the spin-independent Q hoppings only, the bands stay doubly degenerate at every k point. The control gives the on-site spin-orbit coupling, the antisymmetric spin-orbit coupling within the chains (\(z_{22}\), \(z_{23}\)), and the hoppings within and between the chains, and computes the expectation value of \(S_z\) along the k path ("local": ["Sz"]), shown in color:

# Te_ctrl.py: illustrative weights (not fitted), with the symmetric and antisymmetric spin-orbit couplings.
Te_ctrl = {
    "samb": {
        "model": "Te",
        "parameter": {
            "z6": 1.0,  # on-site (symmetric) spin-orbit coupling, l.s.
            "z10": -2.0,  # intra-chain hopping.
            "z11": 1.0,  # intra-chain hopping.
            "z22": 0.3,  # intra-chain antisymmetric spin-orbit coupling (sigma_z).
            "z23": 0.3,  # intra-chain antisymmetric spin-orbit coupling (sigma_x, sigma_y).
            "z31": -0.5,  # inter-chain hopping.
        },
    },
    "output": {
        "dispersion": {
            "k_path": "Γ-M-K-Γ|A'-Γ-A-L-H-A",  # A'-Γ-A: k_z and -k_z.
            "k_point": {
                "Γ": "[0,0,0]",
                "A'": "[0,0,-1/2]",
                "M": "[1/2,0,0]",
                "K": "[1/3,1/3,0]",
                "A": "[0,0,1/2]",
                "L": "[1/2,0,1/2]",
                "H": "[1/3,1/3,1/2]",
            },
            "local": ["Sz"],
        },
    },
}

With the antisymmetric spin-orbit coupling, the bands split except at the time-reversal invariant momenta, e.g., \(\Gamma\) and A, where the bands are doubly degenerate (Kramers). The path A’-\(\Gamma\)-A, with A’ = \(-\)A, runs along the chain direction from \(-k_z\) to \(k_z\). Each pair of split bands is polarized along \(z\) with opposite signs (red and blue), and the red and blue are exchanged between \(k_z\) and \(-k_z\): the spin splitting is odd in \(\boldsymbol{k}\) (time reversal maps the state at \(\boldsymbol{k}\) to the one at \(-\boldsymbol{k}\) with the opposite spin). The spin is parallel or anti-parallel to \(\boldsymbol{k}\): the radial spin texture of a chiral crystal.

Checks by symmetry#

tests/test_materials.py creates the three models and gives random weights to all the SAMB. It checks properties that hold for any complete set of SAMB, independent of their numbering, signs, and basis:

  • the number of SAMB, and that all of them are Q or G of the identity irrep;

  • \(H(\boldsymbol{k})\) is hermitian;

  • \(E_n(\boldsymbol{k}) = E_n(\boldsymbol{k} W)\) for every rotation \(W\) of the space group, and \(E_n(\boldsymbol{k}) = E_n(-\boldsymbol{k})\) by the time reversal;

  • the degeneracies at \(\Gamma\) follow the irreps: SrVO\(_3\) four triplets, MoS\(_2\) three singlets and four doublets, Te nine Kramers pairs;

  • no degeneracy remains at a generic \(\boldsymbol{k}\).

They hold also when the group database changes, as long as the SAMB span the same space of Hamiltonians.
